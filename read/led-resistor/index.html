How to Calculate an LED Resistor Value

Size the series resistor that limits the current through an LED without cooking it.

An LED needs a current limit. The resistor in series with the LED drops whatever voltage the LED does not, and that is what keeps the current inside the safe range.

R = (V_supply − V_F − V_other) ÷ I_LED V_F is the LED forward voltage from its datasheet at the intended current; V_other covers any additional drop in the path, such as a driver transistor.

Reading the forward voltage

Worked example

A red LED on a 5 V rail, 10 mA target.

R = (5 − 1.8) ÷ 0.010 = 320 Ω → choose 330 Ω Resistor power = 3.2 V × 0.0097 A = 31 mW, so any 0603 part is fine. At 5.25 V the current rises to 10.5 mA, still inside a red LED rating.

Running from a higher supply

The same LED on 12 V needs a much larger resistor, and the wasted power grows quickly.

R = (12 − 1.8) ÷ 0.010 = 1020 Ω → choose 1 kΩ P = 10.2 V × 10 mA = 102 mW → use a 1/4 W part A constant-current driver is usually the better answer above about 5 V, because it keeps the brightness constant as the supply changes.
Note Never drive an LED straight from a supply. The forward voltage has a negative temperature coefficient, so the current rises as the junction heats - a feedback loop that ends with the LED destroyed.

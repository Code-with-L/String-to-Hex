How to Calculate RC Time Constants

Predict how long a capacitor takes to charge, discharge, or filter a signal.

The time constant of a resistor and a capacitor is the time it takes the voltage across the capacitor to reach 63.2% of its final value (or to fall to 36.8% when discharging). After five time constants the capacitor is effectively at its final value.

τ = R × C V_C(t) = V_final × (1 − e^(−t/τ)) charging V_C(t) = V_initial × e^(−t/τ) discharging R in ohms and C in farads give τ in seconds. 10 kΩ × 100 µF = 1 s.

The useful fractions

TimeChargedDischarged
1 τ63.2%36.8%
2 τ86.5%13.5%
3 τ95.0%5.0%
5 τ99.3%0.7%
10 τ99.995%0.005%

Worked example

A 10 kΩ resistor charges a 100 µF capacitor from 0 V towards 5 V.

τ = 10 000 × 100 × 10⁻⁶ = 1 s V_C(1 s) = 5 × 0.632 = 3.16 V V_C(5 s) = 5 × 0.993 = 4.96 V The initial charge current is V/R = 5 ÷ 10 000 = 0.5 mA, falling to 1/e of that after one time constant.

Using the time constant as a filter

The corner frequency of an RC low-pass is set by the same product.

f_c = 1 ÷ (2πRC) 10 kΩ and 100 µF give f_c = 159 Hz.
Note Electrolytic capacitors leak, and a real capacitor has an equivalent series resistance, so a slow RC network does not follow the ideal curve exactly. Keep the time constant below about R × C/10 for the ideal model to hold well.

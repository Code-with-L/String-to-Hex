Capacitor

How a capacitor stores charge, how reactance works, and how to combine caps in series and parallel.

A capacitor stores electric charge between two close conductors separated by an insulating dielectric material.

C = Q ÷ V Q is charge in coulombs, V is voltage across the plates. C is capacitance in farads.

Capacitance of a parallel-plate capacitor

The capacitance of two parallel plates grows with plate area and shrinks with the distance between them. The constant ε₀ is the permittivity of free space.

C = ε₀ · εᵣ · A ÷ d ε₀ ≈ 8.854×10⁻¹² F/m; εᵣ is the dielectric constant of the insulator (air 1, SiO₂ 3.9, mica ≈ 7).

Capacitors in series and parallel

Series: 1 ÷ C_T = 1 ÷ C₁ + 1 ÷ C₂ + … Parallel: C_T = C₁ + C₂ + …

Series capacitors all carry the same charge but see a fraction of the total voltage; parallel capacitors all see the same voltage but share the current.

Reactance: why it blocks DC

At zero or very low frequency a capacitor’s impedance is extremely high — it passes no DC after the initial charging spike. As frequency rises its opposition falls.

X_C = 1 ÷ (2πfC) Impedance of a pure capacitor, in ohms.
  • f = 0 HzX_C → ∞ — a capacitor blocks DC entirely
  • f = 50 Hz, C = 100 µFX_C ≈ 32 Ω
  • f = 1 kHz, C = 100 nFX_C ≈ 1.6 kΩ

Energy stored in a charged capacitor

E = ½ · C · V² Energy is released as a destructive pulse when a shorted charged capacitor discharges — which is why large filter caps can explode under fault.
  • C = 10 µF, V = 25 VE = 3.125 mJ
  • C = 470 µF, V = 400 VE ≈ 37.6 J — dangerous
Note Short a charged capacitor — or discharge it with a screwdriver — and the released energy decides whether the resulting flash is exciting or expensive. Always discharge through a resistor, not a short.

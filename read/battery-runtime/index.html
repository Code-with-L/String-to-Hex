How to Calculate Battery Runtime and Capacity

Turn amp-hours into hours of run time, and check the load against the C-rating.

A battery is rated in amp-hours (Ah) or watt-hours (Wh). Run time is that capacity divided by the current the load draws, after allowing for the fact that a battery is never fully empty.

runtime (h) = capacity (Ah) × usable fraction ÷ load current (A) energy (Wh) = capacity (Ah) × nominal voltage (V) Use about 0.8 for lead-acid, which should not be discharged below half charge, and 0.9 to 0.95 for lithium.

Worked example

A 12 V 7 Ah lead-acid battery powers a 1.2 A load.

runtime = 7 × 0.8 ÷ 1.2 = 4.7 hours energy = 7 × 12 = 84 Wh At a 1.2 A load the discharge rate is 1.2 ÷ 7 = 0.17 C, which is gentle and the capacity will be close to the rating.

The C-rating

The C-rating says how fast a battery may be discharged. A 1 C discharge empties the battery in one hour; 0.05 C takes twenty hours. Lead-acid loses a large part of its rated capacity at high discharge rates, so the rating only holds at the rated rate.

discharge rate (C) = load current (A) ÷ capacity (Ah) The same 7 Ah battery gives roughly 5.5 hours at 0.2 C but only about 3.5 hours at 1 C.

Charging

Charging takes longer than discharging. A typical lead-acid charger supplies 0.1 C and is considered full after roughly 1.5 times the discharge time, because the final phase is a slow absorption taper.

Note Capacity is measured at a specific discharge rate, temperature and cutoff voltage. Quoting a figure from the datasheet without those conditions is how runtime estimates end up optimistic by a factor of two.

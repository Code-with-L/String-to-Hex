Trigonometry Cheat Sheet

Sines, cosines and tangents — definitions, special angles, and the identities you reach for most.

Trigonometry is the study of relationships between the sides and angles of triangles — and, beyond that, between angles and circular motion in general.

Definitions in a right triangle

sin α = a ÷ c cos α = b ÷ c tan α = a ÷ b a = opposite, b = adjacent, c = hypotenuse.

Core identities

  • Pythagoreansin²θ + cos²θ = 1 — for every angle
  • Symmetrysin(−θ) = −sin θ, cos(−θ) = cos θ — sine is odd, cosine is even
  • Complementarysin(90° − θ) = cos θ, cos(90° − θ) = sin θ
  • Double anglesin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ cos 2θ = cos²θ − sin²θ = 2cos²θ − 1 = 1 − 2sin²θ
  • Sum and differencesin(α±β) = sin α cos β ± cos α sin β cos(α±β) = cos α cos β ∓ sin α sin β
  • Reciprocalscsc θ = 1÷sin θ, sec θ = 1÷cos θ, cot θ = 1÷tan θ

Law of sines and cosines

a ÷ sin α = b ÷ sin β = c ÷ sin γ c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos γ Use the sine law for AAS, ASA and SSA; the cosine law for SAS and SSS.

Euler’s formula

e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ sin θ = (e^(iθ) − e^(−iθ)) ÷ 2i cos θ = (e^(iθ) + e^(−iθ)) ÷ 2

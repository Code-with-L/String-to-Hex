Electrical Units

Every electrical unit — ampere, volt, ohm, watt, farad, henry, siemens — with its symbol and the quantity it measures.

A unit is a fixed reference quantity. Seventeen SI base and derived units cover electricity and magnetism — each one answers “one what?” for a single physical quantity.

Prefixes and what they mean

  • pico (p)×10⁻¹² — one trillionth
  • nano (n)×10⁻⁹ — one billionth
  • micro (μ)×10⁻⁶ — one millionth
  • milli (m)×10⁻³ — one thousandth
  • kilo (k)×10³ — one thousand
  • mega (M)×10⁶ — one million
  • giga (G)×10⁹ — one billion
  • tera (T)×10¹² — one trillion

Reading a meter like a bench

An ammeter connects in series (it reads the current through it). A voltmeter connects in parallel (it reads the voltage across it). An ohmmeter is self-powered — it des-energises the circuit and measures how hard the loop fights a test current.

Note The symbols on the meter are exactly the symbols printed on the page you are reading — V, A, Ω, Hz, F. If the reading is tiny, milli/k/m/M change the scale, not the quantity.

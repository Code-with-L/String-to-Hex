How to Calculate Op-Amp Amplifier Gain

Set the gain of an inverting or non-inverting amplifier and check the bandwidth and swing limits.

An op-amp has very high open-loop gain, so the closed-loop gain is set almost entirely by the feedback network around it. Two topologies cover most uses.

Non-inverting amplifier

Gain = 1 + R_f ÷ R₁ The input impedance is very high, so the source is barely loaded. Gain cannot go below 1.

Inverting amplifier

Gain = −R_f ÷ R_in The input impedance is R_in, so pick it large enough not to load the source - 10 kΩ is a common starting point for audio.

Worked example

A non-inverting stage with R₁ = 1 kΩ and R_f = 9 kΩ has a gain of 10. A 50 mV signal becomes 500 mV.

Gain = 1 + 9 000 ÷ 1 000 = 10 The equivalent inverting version is R_f = 9 kΩ and R_in = 1 kΩ, but its input impedance is only 1 kΩ.

Bandwidth and slew rate

bandwidth = GBW ÷ noise gain slew rate needed = 2π × f × V_peak With a gain of 10 and a 1 MHz gain-bandwidth product, the closed-loop bandwidth is 100 kHz. A 100 kHz sine at 5 V peak needs 3.1 V/µs of slew rate.

Output swing and headroom

The output cannot quite reach the rails: budget a few hundred millivolts of headroom at light loads, more at heavy ones. The input common-mode range must also include the signal, which rules out many single-supply designs unless the signal is biased around mid-supply.

Note Keep the feedback resistors below about 100 kΩ. Op-amp input bias current flowing through very large resistors creates offset error and noise, and the noise contribution grows with resistance.

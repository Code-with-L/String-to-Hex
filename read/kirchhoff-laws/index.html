Kirchhoff's Laws

KVL and KCL — the two conservation rules that make any multi-loop circuit solvable by hand.

Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL) states that the sum of voltage drops around any closed loop is zero. Kirchhoff's current law (KCL) states that the sum of currents at any node is zero.

Neither law invents physics; both are restatements of energy and charge conservation. KVL follows from the fact that a charge cannot accumulate energy as it goes round a loop; KCL follows from the fact that charge cannot pile up at a junction.

Kirchhoff's voltage law

Σ V_k = 0 Algebraic sum around a closed loop. Rises positive, drops negative.

Pick any closed path through the circuit, including through sources where you traverse from − to + and gain voltage. Write an equation. Repeat for every independent loop. The result is a system of simultaneous equations.

Kirchhoff's current law

Σ I_k = 0 Algebraic sum at a node. Currents entering are positive, leaving negative.

Draw a boundary around any node or junction. Everything the boundary encloses is either a component or a wire, neither of which stores charge, so the current crossing the boundary must sum to zero.

Solving a network

  • Label every current and every voltage in the diagram.
  • Apply KCL at each node except one — the last is implied by the others.
  • Apply KVL around each independent loop; the number of independent loops is branches minus nodes plus one.
  • Solve the simultaneous equations, then check the answer against a law you did not use.
Note The most common mistake is a sign error: traversing a source from + to − loses voltage, and traversing a resistor in the assumed current direction loses IR. Sketch the assumed directions before writing equations.

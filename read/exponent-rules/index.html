Exponent Rules

Every law of exponents — product, quotient, power-of-power, zero, negative and fractional — with worked examples.

An exponent says how many times a base is multiplied by itself: xⁿ means x written n times as a factor.

The three core laws

xᵃ · xᵇ = xᵃ⁺ᵇ xᵃ ÷ xᵇ = xᵃ⁻ᵇ (xᵃ)ᵇ = xᵃ·ᵇ Product, quotient, and power-of-power.
  • xᵃ · xᵇwhen bases are the same, add the exponents
  • xᵃ ÷ xᵇwhen bases are the same, subtract the exponents
  • (xᵃ)ᵇpower raised to a power — multiply the exponents
  • x⁰ = 1any non-zero number to the zero power is 1
  • x⁻ᵃ = 1 ÷ xᵃa negative exponent reciprocates
  • x^(a/b) = ᵇ√(xᵃ)a fractional exponent is a root then a power

Powers of ten and zero

10⁰ = 1, 10¹ = 10, 10⁻¹ = 0.1. Each step of one exponent multiplies by 10, which is the whole of the metric prefix system.

10³ × 10⁻⁵ = 10³⁻⁵ = 10⁻² = 0.01

Worked example

Simplify (2³ · 2⁴) ÷ 2².

(2³ · 2⁴) ÷ 2² = 2³⁺⁴⁻² = 2⁵ = 32

Common mistakes

  • (xᵃ)ᵇ ≠ xᵃ⁺ᵇ — powers of powers multiply exponents, they do not add.
  • xᵃ · yᵃ = (xy)ᵃ only when the exponent is the same, not the base.
  • A negative exponent is not a negative number: 2⁻³ = 1/8, not −8.

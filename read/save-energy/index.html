Everyday energy

How to Save Energy

Electricity is only part of energy. Gas, petrol and food all sit in the same budget — here is where the whole of it goes.

Energy saving is broader than electricity: it is cutting joules drawn for heating, transport, food and computing, measured in kWh, litres or kg CO₂ depending on what your bill itemises.

Energy in a home is bought as electricity (kWh), gas (kWh), solid fuel (kWh) and transport fuel (litres or gallons). Thinking in joules rather than cash keeps you honest: a litre of petrol is about 10 kWh, so a car that avoids one tank a month has saved ~120 kWh — roughly a month of an LED-lit flat.

E = P × t The same relation applies to any energy: power (kW) times time (h) gives energy in kWh.

Transport — the silent giant

  • One avoided car journey per week is worth hundreds of litres of petrol a year.
  • Keeping tyres at the recommended pressure is worth a few percent of fuel.
  • A 100 kg lighter car uses about 6% less fuel — remove the roof box you never use.
  • Short trips by cold engine cost disproportionately; combine errands.

Food — embodied energy

Every kg of beef is roughly 30 kWh of agricultural energy; a kg of lentils is under 2 kWh. One meat-free day a week is the largest single dietary energy saving most people can make.

Computing & standby

A workstation idling at 90 W all day is over 780 kWh a year. Switch it off, or at least sleep it. A laptop on battery is the efficient default — modern ones sip a few watts.

The triage list

  • Audit first: meter one billing cycle, or use last year’s bills — no spreadsheet, no guesswork.
  • Fix the uncontrolled losses: draughts, loft insulation, hot-water lagging.
  • Replace the always-on waste: standby, old fridges, immersion timers.
  • Then the lifestyle changes that cost nothing.
ΔkWh = (P₁ − P₂) × h × days Swapping a 200 W deck light run 6 h/day for a 30 W LED saves about 370 kWh per year.
Note Energy and money are the same number at fixed prices — the skill is knowing which small changes move that number. Start with the biggest term in the formula, not the most satisfying one.

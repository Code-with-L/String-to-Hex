How to Calculate Power Factor Correction

Size the capacitor that brings a motor or a supply back towards a power factor of one.

Power factor is real power divided by apparent power. A low power factor means the supply carries current that does no useful work, which costs money on bills that bill by kVA and stresses the supply.

PF = P ÷ S S = √(P² + Q²) cos φ = P ÷ S For a single phase: S = V × I. For three phase: S = √3 × V_L × I_L.

Capacitor size

Q_c = P × (tan φ₁ − tan φ₂) C = Q_c ÷ (2πf × V²) for a capacitor across the supply φ₁ is the angle of the original power factor and φ₂ the target. For three phase, C is the per-phase value for a star connection.

Worked example

A 10 kW load at 400 V three phase runs at 0.7 power factor and should be corrected to 0.95.

tan φ₁ = 1.02 (0.7 pf) tan φ₂ = 0.33 (0.95 pf) Q_c = 10 000 × (1.02 − 0.33) = 6.9 kVAr C = 6 900 ÷ (2π × 50 × 400²) = 137 µF per phase in star The current falls from S = √3 × 400 × (10 000 ÷ (400 × √3 × 0.7)) = 20.6 A to 14.4 A - a 30% reduction in cable losses, which are proportional to current squared.
Note Capacitors are voltage sensitive. A capacitor corrected for 400 V will be over-stressed on an 415 V or 440 V supply, so always re-check the capacitor rating against the highest system voltage, not the nominal one.

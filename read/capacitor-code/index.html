How to Read Capacitor Codes

Decode the numbers and letters printed on a capacitor to find its value, tolerance and voltage rating.

Small ceramic capacitors carry a three or four digit code instead of a value. The digits give the capacitance in picofarads, with the number of digits telling you where the decimal point goes.

The number code

  • Three digits: the first two are significant, the third is the number of zeros to add. 104 means 10 followed by 4 zeros = 100 000 pF.
  • Four digits: same rule. 100 nF is written 104, 1 µF is 105, 10 µF is 106.
  • A decimal point anywhere means picofarads directly: 4.7 means 4.7 pF.
C (pF) = digits × 10^multiplier For 104: (1 × 10 + 0) × 10⁴ = 100 000 pF = 100 nF = 0.1 µF.

The letter code

Letter codes replace the digit multiplier for small values, or add a decimal point for larger ones.

CodeMultiplierExample
p or P× 0.14p7 = 4.7 pF
n or N× 14n7 = 4.7 nF
R× 14R7 = 4.7 Ω (resistors only)
µ or u× 1 000 0004µ7 = 4.7 µF
m or M× 0.0014m7 = 4.7 mF

Tolerance and voltage

Ceramic capacitors use letter codes for tolerance: B ±1%, C ±2%, D ±5%, F ±1%, G ±2%, J ±5%, K ±10%, M ±20%. Electrolytics print the tolerance as a percentage and the voltage rating in volts directly.

Worked example

A capacitor marked "104K 50V" is 100 nF with a ±10% tolerance rated for 50 V. The "1" in the code is the first significant digit, "0" the second, "4" means four zeros - so 100 000 pF, which is 100 nF.

Note Electrolytic and tantalic capacitors are polarised. The stripe marks the negative terminal, and reversing them destroys the part. Ceramic capacitors are not polarised and can go either way.

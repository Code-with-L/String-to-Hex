Kilowatt-Hours and Energy

What kWh means, how bills work, and why 1 kWh is 3.6 megajoules of anything.

One kilowatt-hour (kWh) is one thousand watts of power applied for one hour — that is 3.6 megajoules (MJ) of energy, whatever the source.

E = P × t In electrical terms: watts × seconds gives joules; watts × hours gives watt-hours (Wh); kW × h gives kilowatt-hours.

How utility bills work

The meter integrates your power draw over time and records energy in kWh. Multiply the meter reading by your unit rate to get money: a 3 kW immersion heater running 4 hours a day for a year is 4,380 kWh.

Cost = kWh × rate Energy and money are the same number once the meter is right.

Comparing devices

Cost comparisons across devices use the same relation. A 2 kW space heater running 3 hours a day for a year consumes 2,190 kWh; a modern heat-pump clothes dryer with a COP of 3 uses a third of that.

BTU and kWh

US gas utilities bill in therms (100,000 BTU); European gas utilities bill in kWh. Both are the same measurement of heat.

1 therm = 100,000 BTU ≈ 29.3 kWh 1 kWh ≈ 3412.14 BTU
Note Stats quoted different ways in different countries but the physics does not care: 1 kWh is always 3.6 MJ.

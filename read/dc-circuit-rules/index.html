DC Circuit Rules

The handful of formulae every DC problem obeys — series and parallel combination, divider rule, KVL/KCL — in one place.

DC circuit rules are the conservation laws and series/parallel identities that hold in every linear DC network: KVL, KCL, Ohm’s law, and the expressions for equivalent resistance/capacitance/inductance.

Series circuit

V_T = V₁ + V₂ + V₃ + … I_T = I₁ = I₂ = I₃ = … R_T = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + … 1/C_T = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + 1/C₃ + … L_T = L₁ + L₂ + L₃ + …

Parallel circuit

V_T = V₁ = V₂ = V₃ = … I_T = I₁ + I₂ + I₃ + … 1/R_T = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ + … C_T = C₁ + C₂ + C₃ + … 1/L_T = 1/L₁ + 1/L₂ + 1/L₃ + …

Voltage and current divider rules

In a series string, the fraction of the total voltage that drops across Rᵢ is proportional to Rᵢ as a share of the total:

Vᵢ = V_T · Rᵢ ÷ (R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + …)

In a parallel combination, the current through one branch is the share of the total current that the complementary resistances dictate:

I₁ = I_T · (R₂ + R₃ + …) ÷ (R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + …)

Kirchhoff’s laws

  • KVLThe sum of voltage drops around a closed loop is zero: Σ Vᵢ = 0
  • KCLThe sum of currents at a node is zero: Σ Iᵢ = 0

In practical terms: rises prove drops; if you traverse a battery from – to + you add a voltage, if you traverse a resistor in the direction of current you subtract it; outlets = inlets at every node.

Capacitor and inductor energy

E_C = ½ · C · V² E_L = ½ · L · I²

Permittivity and the plates

C = ε₀ · εᵣ · A ÷ d d is the separation between plates; A is the overlapping area.
Note These identities are the reason the triangle works. If you memorise just KVL, KCL and Ohm’s law, you can re-derive each row above when you need it.

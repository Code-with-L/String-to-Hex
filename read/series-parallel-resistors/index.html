How to Calculate Series and Parallel Resistors

Combine resistances in series and in parallel, and know which one you are looking at.

Resistors in series share the same current and their voltages add up. Resistors in parallel share the same voltage and their currents add up. Every combination of resistors is one of these two cases.

Series

R_total = R₁ + R₂ + … The current is identical through every resistor, and the total voltage is the sum of the individual drops.

Parallel

1 ÷ R_total = 1 ÷ R₁ + 1 ÷ R₂ + … R_total = R₁ × R₂ ÷ (R₁ + R₂) The two-resistor shortcut is worth memorising. For three equal resistors in parallel the total is R ÷ 3; for n equal resistors it is R ÷ n.

Worked example

A 1 kΩ and a 2 kΩ resistor are in parallel.

R_total = 1000 × 2000 ÷ (1000 + 2000) = 667 Ω With 12 V across the pair, the current is 12 ÷ 667 = 18 mA. The 1 kΩ resistor carries 12 mA and the 2 kΩ carries 6 mA - the higher resistance carries less.

Mixed networks

Reduce the network one step at a time: find the nearest pair that is plainly in series or plainly in parallel, combine them, and repeat until one resistor is left.

Note Two resistors in parallel always give a smaller value than the smaller of the two, and two in series always give a larger value than the larger of the two. If your answer breaks that rule, the topology was misread.

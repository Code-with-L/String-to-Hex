How to Read a Resistor Colour Code

Read the value, multiplier and tolerance straight off the bands.

Resistor values are printed as coloured bands because the numbers would be too small to read on a tiny package. The first bands give the significant digits, the third gives the multiplier, the fourth gives the tolerance.

Four bands and five bands

R = (d₁ × 10 + d₂) × multiplier For a five-band part: R = (d₁ × 100 + d₂ × 10 + d₃) × multiplier.

A four-band resistor is digit, digit, multiplier, tolerance: brown-black-red-gold is 10 × 100 = 1 kΩ ±5%. A five-band resistor has three significant digits: brown-black-black-brown-brown is 100 × 10 = 1 kΩ ±1%.

Worked example

Orange-red-brown-gold: 3, 2, ×100, ±5% → 32 × 100 = 3 200 Ω ±5%, which is 3.2 kΩ ±160 Ω.

The extra bands

  • A gold or silver band separated by a gap is a tolerance band on a precision metal-film resistor.
  • A brown band at the far end is the temperature coefficient: 100 ppm/°C.
  • A red band in the fourth position on a 5-band part is a "fail-safe" indicator rather than a multiplier.
Note Resistor codes are not the same as capacitor codes. "4R7" is 4.7 Ω on a resistor but 4.7 µF on a capacitor; on a resistor the letter is in the middle of the number, on a capacitor it follows it.

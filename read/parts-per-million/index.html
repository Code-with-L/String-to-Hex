Parts Per Million

What ppm means, how it relates to percent, permil, ppb and ppt, and why Co₂ is measured in it.

A part-per-million is exactly what it says: one part in a million, so 1 ppm = 1e-6. It is the purest way to write very small fractions without scientific notation.

The ppm family in one line

1 ratio = 1 % = 10 ‰ = 10,000 ppm = 10,000,000 ppb = 10,000,000,000 ppt

ppm by mass (ppmw) and by volume (ppmv)

  • ppmw — the ratio of weights: mg/L of water roughly equal ppm (for dilute solutions at 4 °C).
  • ppmv — the ratio of volumes: for gases, "ppm of CO₂" means parts per million by volume (µmol/mol).
  • For air, ppmv ≈ ppmv in dry air at standard pressure. At altitude the absolute number of molecules changes, but ratio does not.

ppm and molarity (aqueous solutions)

Molarity is moles per litre. To convert to ppm in water you almost always need the solute’s molar mass and the density of water at the actual temperature — cold water is denser, so the same molarity is a smaller ppm.

mg/L = 1000 × M × MM ppm ≈ mg/L (1 mg/L = 1 ppm at 25 °C)

Frequency stability

A quartz crystal specified to 10 ppm is off by at most 10 parts per million — that is 36 seconds over a year, or better than your phone’s clock. It is also how tolerances are often quoted for voltage references and resistors ("0.1 % = 1,000 ppm").

ppm, ppb and ppt

  • ppb = 1,000 ppm — water with 0.1 ppm arsenic is 100 ppb.
  • ppt = 1,000 ppb — used for CO₂ seawater, mercury, contamination.
  • A 0.001% leakage = 10 ppm; a 1 bp = 100 ppm.
Note Calculators on this site use the same convention as the reference page: ratio = 1, and every other notation is a multiple of that ratio.

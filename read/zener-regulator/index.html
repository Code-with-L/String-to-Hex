How to Calculate a Zener Regulator Resistor

Pick the series resistor for a Zener that holds a reference voltage while the load changes.

A Zener diode is designed to break down at a specified voltage. Feeding it through a series resistor makes the resistor set the current, and the Zener holds the output voltage as the load and the input change.

The three currents

  • I_Z - the Zener current. It must stay above the knee current, or the voltage will not be regulated.
  • I_L - the load current drawn from the output.
  • I_R - the current through the series resistor, which is I_Z + I_L.
R_S = (V_in − V_Z) ÷ (I_Z + I_L) P_R = (V_in − V_Z) × I_R P_Z = V_Z × I_Z Choose I_Z with margin: the datasheet knee current is a minimum, and at half the rated power the Zener will not hold a sharp voltage.

Worked example

A 12 V input must feed a 5.1 V Zener into a 20 mA load. Allow 5 mA of Zener current for a stable 5.1 V.

R_S = (12 − 5.1) ÷ (0.020 + 0.005) = 276 Ω → choose 270 Ω I_R = 6.9 ÷ 270 = 25.6 mA, so I_Z = 5.6 mA P_R = 6.9 × 0.0256 = 0.18 W → use a 1/4 W part P_Z = 5.1 × 0.0056 = 29 mW → well inside a 500 mW device This is a shunt regulator: it wastes current, so it suits small loads (tens of milliamps) rather than anything substantial.

Worst cases

Check the design at the extremes of the input range and the load range. The minimum Zener current happens at the lowest input voltage and the highest load; the maximum resistor power happens at the highest input voltage.

Note A Zener regulator only works while the input stays above the Zener voltage plus the headroom the Zener needs - typically a few volts. For more than a few hundred milliamps, use a switching buck converter instead and get 85–95% efficiency rather than the 40% a shunt regulator manages.
